What this page shows

The Monitor replays 24 ICU stays from hospital B of the PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019 one hour at a time. Each bed shows the research score from the promoted model, the alert-policy state, the measurements behind the score and the strongest drivers. The records were sampled with a fixed seed (10 stays that meet the challenge sepsis definition, 14 that do not), so you can see hits, misses and false alarms side by side.

Scores were computed offline by the research pipeline and exported to static files. Nothing here is live patient data. The page has no server and sends nothing anywhere.

How the score and alerts work

  • Target. The supplied SepsisLabel, which turns positive 6 hours before the challenge-defined onset. The onset marker is that first positive hour plus 6.
  • Features. Causal only: the row for hour t uses that stay's measurements up to t. Vital-sign windows, lab values and changes, partial SOFA components, and measurement-pattern features such as hours since a test was last charted.
  • Model. A 3-seed bag of LightGBM models trained on hospital A, calibrated on a separate hospital A slice.
  • Alert policy. A versioned state machine (monitoring, alerted, acknowledged, cooldown, data unavailable) with its threshold chosen on hospital A validation data for a budget of 10 alerts per 100 patient-days.
  • Data unavailable. Hours without any valid vital sign are never shown as low risk.
  • Drivers. TreeSHAP contributions on the log-odds scale. They describe associations the model uses. They are not causes or treatment advice.

Limits

  • Every model lost accuracy at the new hospital. At the chosen alert budget the model warns early for about one septic patient in nine at hospital B.
  • Measurement-pattern features can encode local ordering habits. The top driver, hours since FiO2 was last charted, is a workflow signal, not physiology.
  • The hospital B records were scored in round 1 before the round 2 model was chosen, so round 2 test results are a second look.
